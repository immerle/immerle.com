Sonic analysis

An optional feature (Admin → Settings → Audio analysis) that listens to your library itself: Immerle decodes each track with ffmpeg and measures its tempo, key, energy and timbre, locally, without any external service. Those measures then power a track radio, endless queues, mood playlists, Auto-DJ, new smart playlist rules and duplicate detection.

Everything is off by default. The analysis is the master switch; each feature built on it has its own toggle on top, so you turn on only what you want.

ToggleWhat it does
Analyze the libraryRuns the analysis in the background. Required by every toggle below
Track radio"Start radio" on a track, album or artist; also answers getSimilarSongs for Subsonic clients
AutoplayExtends a queue about to run out with similar tracks
Mood playlistsPublishes shared Calm, Focus, Melancholy, Energy, Party and Workout playlists
Auto-DJPlays a playlist, or reorders the queue, for smooth transitions
Sonic duplicatesLists tracks that sound identical in Admin → Library

Smart playlist rules on BPM, energy, danceability and key are available as soon as the analysis runs; they need no toggle of their own.

The analysis​

  • What it needs: ffmpeg, the same binary used for transcoding (it ships in the Docker image). Without it the section shows a warning and nothing runs.
  • What it decodes: three 30-second excerpts per track (at 20 %, 50 % and 80 % of its length), downmixed to mono. The whole file is never read and no audio is stored.
  • What it measures:
    • tempo (BPM), from the rhythm of the onsets;
    • key and mode (major/minor), also given in Camelot notation (8A, 9B…);
    • energy (0 to 1, from loudness and brightness), danceability (how clear the beat is) and brightness;
    • a sonic fingerprint of 34 values (the measures above plus the timbre) used to compare tracks.
  • Cost: well under a second per track on a desktop CPU, a few seconds on a Raspberry Pi. The first pass over a large library takes hours there; it runs in the background, one track at a time by default (raise Tracks analyzed in parallel on a bigger machine), pauses during library scans and resumes after a restart. Progress shows in the same settings section.
  • Storage: about 200 bytes per track (around 20 MB for 100,000 tracks).
  • When it re-runs: a track is analyzed again when its file changes, or when an Immerle update improves the analysis. A file that can't be analyzed is counted as failed and not retried until one of those happens.

Tracks not downloaded yet (on-demand results) have nothing to decode: they join the analysis once they're in your library.

Track radio​

"Start radio" from a track's menu, or the radio button on an album or artist page, plays tracks that sound like it: the closest matches in the library, at most two per artist, with a little randomness so two radios from the same track differ. From a track, that track plays first.

Subsonic clients get it too: getSimilarSongs / getSimilarSongs2 (the "instant mix" of Symfonium, DSub and others) use the sonic radio once the seed is analyzed, and fall back to tracks of the same genre otherwise.

Autoplay​

When fewer than three tracks are left in the queue, the player adds 20 that sound like the last five played, so the music never stops. It stays out of the way on internet radio, while following a Jam, with repeat on, and on a device that only mirrors another one. Each listener can turn it off under Settings in the app once the admin has enabled it.

Mood playlists​

Six shared, public playlists, refreshed daily with the other auto-generated playlists and shown in the Home screen's "made for you" row:

PlaylistPicks
Calmthe least energetic tracks, not too danceable
Focusmid energy, little pulse
Melancholyminor key, low energy
Energythe most energetic tracks, 115 BPM and up
Partyvery danceable, energetic, 100 to 135 BPM
Workoutenergetic, 120 to 160 BPM

Energy and danceability thresholds are relative to your own library (Calm is the calmest part of your music), each playlist holds up to 100 tracks with at most three per artist, and a mood with fewer than 15 matching tracks gets no playlist. Turning the toggle off removes them.

Auto-DJ​

Orders tracks so each one flows into the next: close tempos (a half or double tempo counts as close), compatible keys on the Camelot wheel, and no sudden energy jumps. Two ways to use it:

  • on a playlist, the Auto-DJ button next to Play plays it in that order (the playlist itself is unchanged);
  • in the queue, Auto-DJ reorders everything after the current track, which keeps playing.

Tracks not analyzed yet are kept at the end, in their original order. It pairs well with the crossfade.

Smart playlist rules​

Smart playlists gain Energy and Danceability (0 to 100), Key (Camelot) and Mode rules, plus energy and danceability sorts. BPM now falls back on the analyzed tempo when a file has no BPM tag (the tag wins when present). A track not analyzed yet matches none of these rules.

Sonic duplicates​

Admin → Library → Probable duplicates lists groups of tracks that sound identical and last about as long: the same recording under other tags, or in another format. In each group the best copy (highest bitrate, then biggest file) is marked to keep; the others can be deleted from there. Nothing is deleted automatically. A different version of a song (radio edit, live) is not a duplicate.

Limits​

  • Tempo can read as half or double the real one (85 instead of 170). Auto-DJ treats those as matching, but a BPM smart rule may miss the track.
  • Energy is an estimate from loudness and brightness, not a model trained on listening tests: a quiet but intense track can read as calm.
  • Voice and instruments aren't detected, so there's no "instrumental" mood; Focus is about steadiness, not the absence of vocals.
